# Glossary

Terminology used across this specification set. Dutch market terms are given with their English
working equivalent; the **bold** form is the one used in code and UI copy.

## Market & regulatory

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Grootverbruik** (GV) | Large-consumption connection. Electricity: connection capacity above 3×80 A. Gas: above 40 m³/h. GV connections are metered per interval and settled on measured data, not on a profile. This is PeakPower's segment. |
| Kleinverbruik (KV) | Small-consumption connection. Out of scope. |
| **EAN** | The 18-digit GS1 code identifying a *metering point* (`aansluiting`/connection point). Stable across physical meter replacement. The platform's natural key for a connection. |
| GLN-13 | 13-digit GS1 Global Location Number identifying a *market party* (used for PVNed sender/receiver identification). |
| **DSO** (`netbeheerder`) | Distribution System Operator — Liander, Enexis, Stedin, etc. Owns the connection and the meter. |
| **TSO** | Transmission System Operator — TenneT in the Netherlands. Operates the imbalance settlement. |
| **BRP** (`programmaverantwoordelijke`, PV) | Balance Responsible Party. Submits energy programmes to TenneT and carries the imbalance risk for its portfolio. **Also a platform record [DEC-69]**: reference data holding one party's credentials, endpoint, document format and ingestion adapter. A metering point is assigned to a BRP. **PVNed is the first BRP, not the only one** — its webhook, parser and validation path are *one adapter behind a port*, not the ingestion pipeline itself, while raw-payload persistence, versioning **[DEC-07]** and quarantine stay BRP-agnostic in the pipeline. Cost: an interface seam and a `brp` table now, so a second adapter later is additive. |
| **Allocation** (`allocatie`) | The process of assigning measured volumes per interval to a BRP portfolio. PVNed sends allocation/realisation data to PeakPower. |
| **Reconciliation** (`reconciliatie`) | Later correction of allocated volumes against final metered data. PVNed **does** supply reconciliation data after the 10-working-day correction window **[DEC-98]**, sometimes as a manual process rather than a document on the feed; manual entry **[DEC-60]** covers that case. A correction that changes an already-invoiced volume produces a **correction invoice** for the delta, whenever it arrives **[DEC-99]**. |
| **Imbalance** (`onbalans`) | The deviation between a BRP's programme and its realised position in a settlement period, priced by TenneT. **Out of scope for charging [DEC-25]** — PVNed `A12` documents are stored but not turned into an invoice line, and no allocation method is stated in the customer contract. The definition stands; only the charging does not. |
| **ISP** / MTU | Imbalance Settlement Period / Market Time Unit — 15 minutes. |
| **Energiebelasting** (EB) | Dutch energy tax. Levied per EAN per calendar year on net consumed volume **[DEC-22]**, with **degressive** tiers: the rate per kWh falls as annual volume rises. Tier crossings are the reason a recalculation is required — see [Annual true-up](../40-processes/05-annual-true-up.md). ~~**Deferred by [DEC-24]**: invoice line 5 is not implemented for now and the annual true-up is deferred with it.~~ ⚠ **Reversed 2026-08-19 by [DEC-74]** — EB is **back in scope**, which the definition above always said it had to be. What the platform now holds: a **versioned, editable bracket table** (tier boundaries and €/kWh rates per year), a **per-customer reduction or exemption** for the minority who do not pay the standard rate (growers are the named example), calculation per EAN per calendar year on net usage, and a **ledger push** of the result to the **bookkeeping program** **[DEC-76]**. `IEnergyTaxCalculator` and `billing.energy_tax_tariff` are implemented rather than left unpopulated. When an EAN transfers between customers mid-year, **each period gets 50% of each bracket** — a straight half-and-half split of the annual tier boundaries, not a pro-rata by days ([OQ-77] closed). A correction landing after a month is finalised produces a **correction invoice** at any time **[DEC-99]**. ⚠ Whether the **vermindering** applies is not decided — see [OQ-96]. |
| **Vermindering** | The fixed annual reduction on energiebelasting, credited per connection rather than per kWh. ⚠ **Not decided.** **[DEC-74]** brings energiebelasting back in scope with brackets and per-customer reductions but is silent on the vermindering, which was part of [OQ-14]'s original question. It is a fixed annual credit per connection, so it changes the amount on every affected invoice — see [OQ-96]. |
| ODE | Opslag Duurzame Energie — a separate levy until 2022, since merged into energiebelasting. Retained here only as a historical note. |
| **SJV** (`standaardjaarverbruik`) | Standard annual consumption for a connection: the market's reference estimate of what a connection uses in a year, spread over intervals by profile fractions. In this platform it is a **sanity check, not a source** — whether a metering point produces is the **customer's declaration at onboarding [DEC-112]**, and SJV plus fractions exist only to check that declaration against something. |
| **Gas** | The second commodity. **Out of scope [DEC-68]** — for now, not permanently. Electricity is the only commodity with data, tariffs or products, and no gas-specific pricing, unit or tariff work is done. **[DEC-15] stands**: the `commodity` discriminator on metering point, product, tariff and price stays in the model, because it is nearly free now and expensive to retrofit. ~~When it enters scope it keeps **the same EAN model and the same block products**; only pricing and units differ, and **gas volumes are in m³**, not kWh **[DEC-30]**.~~ ⚠ **Reversed 2026-08-19 by [DEC-68]** — [DEC-30] is withdrawn, not implemented; the m³ unit and the gas block products return only when gas does. ⚠ The **calorific correction** question (m³ → kWh) ~~[OQ-87]~~ **closes as not applicable** while gas is out of scope, and reopens with gas. |
| **Day-ahead price** | The wholesale price per MTU resulting from the day-ahead auction (EPEX SPOT NL), in **€/MWh**. Applied to volume not covered by a purchased block, and to unused block cover on the sale side **[DEC-23]**. Used **raw, with no spread [DEC-44]**. ~~Not applied to physically exported volume, which settles at the **feed-in tariff** instead.~~ ⚠ **Reversed 2026-08-19 by [DEC-87]** — physically exported volume is credited at the **day-ahead price** as well, raw; there is no feed-in tariff. The NL curve arrives at **18:00 Europe/Amsterdam [DEC-36]**, and Montel's **day-ahead history is available for backfill [DEC-75]**, so positions can be settled retrospectively to whatever depth the licence allows and there is no backfill cliff. |
| **Base load** (`base`) | A product delivering constant power across every hour of the delivery period, 24/7. |
| **Peak load** (`peak`) | A product delivering constant power during **peak hours** only, and nothing outside them. Volume is counted against the peak calendar for the commodity and year **[DEC-14]**, whose exclusion list is empty for electricity **[DEC-19]**. See **Peak hours**. |
| **Peak hours** | Monday to Friday, at or after **08:00** and strictly before **20:00** Europe/Amsterdam. **Public holidays are not excluded** — a holiday falling on a weekday is a peak day **[DEC-19]**, matching the exchange convention for Dutch power peak-load products, so the platform's peak profile agrees with the product PeakPower hedges in. |
| **Off-peak** | Every hour that is not a peak hour. |
| **Cal** / Q / M | Trading shorthand for calendar-year, quarter and month delivery periods (e.g. *Cal-27*, *Q1-27*, *Aug-26*). |
| **Clip** | A whole-MW tradeable unit. Wholesale blocks trade in integer MW, while a customer may request from **0,01 MW in steps of 0,01 MW [DEC-70]** — the gap between the two is what PeakPower carries. |
| **BTW** | Dutch VAT. All platform prices, wallet balances and reservations are VAT-**exclusive**; VAT is added at invoice level **[DEC-26]**, at **21% on every line category, with no exemptions and no reverse-charge cases [DEC-64]**. ⚠ That rate is recorded as *stated*, not as advised — a customer outside the standard rate reopens it. ⚠ **Amended 2026-08-19 by [DEC-76]** — the platform now computes **no VAT at all**. It pushes ex-VAT amounts against a **ledger account** and the **bookkeeping program** applies that account's rate, which is set per ledger number. **[DEC-64]** survives only as the **reference rate**, because **[DEC-78]** needs one. ⚠ **Closed by [DEC-78]** — ~~whether the wallet debit settles the ex-VAT subtotal or the inclusive total is still open — [OQ-83]~~: a trade reservation and its debit are **VAT-inclusive**, `volume × price × (1 + VAT rate)`, while prices stay quoted and stored ex-VAT. The sentence above therefore holds for prices and balances but no longer for **reservations**. |

## Platform concepts

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Customer** | The contracting **company**. Holds the commercial relationship: legal identity, KvK registration, VAT number, **bank accounts** — added and deactivated, never edited **[DEC-71]** — addresses and contacts. Owns exactly one wallet and one or more metering points. Used interchangeably with *customer company* where the distinction from an account matters. |
| **Customer account** | One person's login at a customer company: username, name, role in the company, contact details. A customer has **one or more** accounts, created by a PeakPower employee. ~~All accounts of one customer have **identical privileges** and see identical data **[DEC-16]**.~~ ⚠ **Amended 2026-08-19 by [DEC-71]** — accounts now carry an **admin** flag, the smallest possible role model: exactly two levels, and it exists only to make **four-eyes approval** expressible. Everything **[DEC-16]** said about *who creates accounts* is unchanged, and data visibility is still identical. |
| **Admin account** | A customer account carrying the **admin** flag **[DEC-71]**. The only role distinction inside a customer company. When the company has four-eyes **enabled**, a sensitive action taken by one admin must be approved — or declined — by a **different** admin of the same company; see **Four-eyes approval** for the action list. Admins are created by PeakPower employees like every other account **[DEC-16]**, and **[DEC-17]** (every action records the acting account) is what makes the approval trail meaningful. |
| **Customer user** | The person behind a customer account. Used when talking about behaviour and needs; *customer account* is used when talking about the record, the login, or attribution. |
| **Role in the company** | The account holder's job title — *Energy Manager*, *Finance Director*, *Operations*. **Descriptive only.** It appears next to their name in the audit trail for context; it grants nothing. Not to be confused with a platform role. |
| **Acting account** | The specific customer account that performed an action. Recorded on every trade event and wallet movement, so history shows not only *which company* but *which person* **[DEC-17]**. |
| **Metering point** | A platform record wrapping an EAN, with customer-supplied name and description, commodity, validity period, and the **BRP** it is assigned to **[DEC-69]**. Whether the connection **produces** is the customer's declaration at onboarding **[DEC-112]**, checked against **SJV** and profile fractions rather than derived from them. The property still defaults to `UNKNOWN` and `UNKNOWN` is still treated as `EXPECTED` for completeness alerting **[F02-R32]**; a change to it reads forward only **[F01-R41]**. |
| **Interval reading** | One 15-minute measurement for one metering point, in one direction (consumption or production), from one data version. |
| **Delivery date** | The Amsterdam calendar day a measurement belongs to. 96 intervals normally; **92** on the spring-forward day, **100** on the autumn fall-back day. |
| **Data version** | One BRP document for a metering point and delivery date — a PVNed document today, since PVNed is the first BRP and versioning is **BRP-agnostic [DEC-69]**, **[DEC-07]**. Later versions supersede earlier ones; the newest received version is authoritative. |
| **Final data** | The state of a delivery date after the PVNed correction window (10 working days) closes and no newer version has arrived. ⚠ **Amended 2026-08-19 by [DEC-98]** — "final" is a status, not a guarantee: reconciliation data does arrive after the window, sometimes as a manual process, so the window is no longer the end of the correction story. A correction that lands later produces a **correction invoice** for the delta at any time **[DEC-99]**. |
| **Price indication** | A non-binding market price shown to the customer, sourced from Montel. Never a quote. **Must not be displayed publicly [DEC-27]**; display inside the authenticated portal is permitted. ⚠ **Amended 2026-08-19 by [DEC-80]** — it is **never shown raw**: the customer sees the **market bid** plus a **configurable percentage**, reference data with a default of **2%**, not a constant. A price the platform gives is a **bid, and firm only when PeakPower says so**. ⚠ Which side of the market carries the markup is not settled — OQ-25's comment says *bid* + percentage, OQ-23's answer says *ask* + 2%; the comment governs and the wording is carried on [OQ-23] together with the missing Montel ticker symbols. ~~Customer CSV export is not covered by that permission and is treated as not permitted — see [OQ-24].~~ ⚠ **Amended 2026-08-19 by [DEC-81]** — customers may see forward prices in the portal, but the board shows the **current curve only**: **no history and no export** — no CSV, no download, no API **[DEC-97]**. Licence-driven, not a product choice. |
| **Trade request** | A customer's request to buy or sell a block: product, period, direction, and a volume per metering point. Raised by one customer account; **any** account of the same customer may answer the resulting offer. |
| **Offer** | PeakPower's firm, time-limited price response to a trade request. It is the one price the platform gives that **is** firm: an indication is a bid, firm only when PeakPower says so **[DEC-80]**. |
| **Reaction window** | The period during which an offer can be accepted. Default 30 minutes; set per offer. |
| **Block** (`energy block`) | A confirmed position: base or peak, a delivery period, a volume in MW, an agreed price, allocated across one or more metering points. A block runs to the end of its **delivery period whatever happens to the customer's contract [DEC-82]**: once the contract ends there is no metering data, therefore no covered volume, therefore the **entire** block volume is over-coverage and is sold at the day-ahead price **[DEC-23]**. Offboarding does not unwind, transfer or mark-to-market anything ([OQ-29] closed). |
| **Allocation (of a block)** | The split of a block's MW across the metering points named in the trade request. Not to be confused with market *allocation* above. Allocations are multiples of **0,01 MW [DEC-70]**, so the non-whole-MW tail is real. |
| **Short sale** | Selling a block the customer does not hold for the period. **Permitted [DEC-72]**, reversing **[DEC-34]**; the case that motivates it is a customer with solar production selling expected surplus. The sell path no longer validates against confirmed holdings for the period. ⚠ A short carries market risk the prepaid wallet **[AS-11]** does not cover, because a short is a promise to deliver rather than a spend, so the pre-trade balance check **[DEC-41]** does not bound it. No collateral or exposure rule is decided — see [OQ-94]. |
| **Net usage** | **Consumption minus production**, for one interval at one metering point. **May be negative** when production exceeds consumption in that interval, which is **feed-in**. This is the platform's volume basis for coverage, the net position and invoicing **[DEC-22]**, superseding **[AS-06]**. The consumption chart shows it alongside the two gross series, never instead of them. A customer can also retrieve it **programmatically** — interval and aggregated, per metering point, scoped to the calling company **[DEC-97]**. Nothing priced travels with it: forward prices and price indications stay out of that surface **[DEC-27]**, **[DEC-81]**. Whether the transport is an API, file/FTP or both is not chosen — see [OQ-95]. |
| **Coverage** | For a given interval, the share of **net usage** supplied by purchased blocks **[DEC-22]**. Unchanged by **[DEC-44]** — coverage is measured before the sale leg splits, so an interval of feed-in is simply an interval with no net usage to cover. |
| **Uncovered volume** | **Net usage** in excess of block coverage, priced at day-ahead. Purchase side only; unaffected by **[DEC-44]**. |
| **Over-coverage** | Block volume in excess of **net usage** in an interval **[DEC-22]**. ⚠ **Split by [DEC-44]**, and the distinction is a money one. *Unused block cover* — bought volume the customer did not consume — is credited at the **day-ahead price** as a separate sale line **[DEC-23]**. ~~*Physically exported* volume — net usage below zero — is **feed-in**, and is credited at the **feed-in tariff** on its own line instead.~~ ⚠ **Reversed 2026-08-19 by [DEC-87]** — exported volume is credited at the **day-ahead price** too, raw, exactly as unused cover is **[DEC-23]**; the feed-in tariff and its own line category are withdrawn, and no topup and no feed-in fee touch export volume. The two are still never netted against each other or against the purchase lines — three volumes, three times — but they now carry **one price**, not two. |
| **Feed-in** | Physically **exported** energy: the part of net usage that falls below zero in an interval, `max(−U, 0)`. ~~Its own invoice line category — **line 6** — settled at the **feed-in tariff**, not at day-ahead **[DEC-44]**.~~ ⚠ **Reversed 2026-08-19 by [DEC-87]** — settled **raw at the day-ahead price** for the interval; there is no feed-in tariff, no feed-in fee and no separate line category. Still distinct from *over-coverage*, which is bought volume that went unused; the two can occur in the same month, at different times — now at the same price. |
| ~~**Feed-in tariff**~~ | ⚠ **Reversed 2026-08-19 by [DEC-87]** — **withdrawn. There is no feed-in tariff.** Export settles raw at the day-ahead price **[DEC-23]**, so there is no per-customer rate to resolve, no `MISSING_FEED_IN_TARIFF` condition and no invoice-run skip. [OQ-86] closes with it: the €662,53 fallback question disappears, because there is no tariff to fail to resolve. Original definition, kept readable: *the per-unit rate at which exported energy is credited to the customer, in €/kWh applied directly to the kWh volume with no divisor **[DEC-44]**, **[DEC-35]**; per-customer, per-period reference data, signed, resolved per interval so a mid-month change produced two lines rather than a blended rate, never edited retroactively into an invoiced period.* |
| **Wallet** | The customer's single prepaid money account. Holds a settled balance and a reserved amount. ⚠ **Amended 2026-08-19 by [DEC-77]** — it funds **trading only**: reservation on request, debit on execution, all inside the wallet. A customer can only trade within their balance, which is what makes **[AS-11]** (no negative balance) hold without a credit concept. **Delivery invoices are never settled from it** — monthly day-ahead, export and energiebelasting amounts go to the **bookkeeping program** as a draft invoice **[DEC-88]** and are paid to the bank. This **reverses [AS-12]**, and the `INVOICE_DEBIT` ledger entry type is removed. There are **no balance thresholds and no low-balance alerts [DEC-90]**: the balance is visible, not monitored, and the pre-trade check **[DEC-41]** is the only thing that reads it for a decision. |
| **Ledger** | The append-only record of every wallet movement, each entry carrying the resulting balances. ⚠ **Amended 2026-08-19 by [DEC-77]** — `INVOICE_DEBIT` is removed; the wallet ledger carries trading movements, deposits and withdrawals only. Not to be confused with the **ledger accounts** of the bookkeeping program, which is where the platform pushes energiebelasting **[DEC-74]** and invoice amounts ex-VAT **[DEC-76]**. |
| **Reservation** | An amount held against the wallet for a trade that is accepted but not yet confirmed — ~~or, above the threshold, **accepted but not yet approved [DEC-33]**~~ ⚠ **Reversed 2026-08-19 by [DEC-71]**: there is no threshold, so under four-eyes *every* trade of that company waits for a second admin, and without four-eyes none does. Reduces available balance without changing the settled balance. Taken at acceptance in the same transaction whichever state the trade lands in, and released in full by any exit other than confirmation. ~~Sized at the full trade value ex-VAT **[AS-10]**, **[DEC-26]**, with no buffer **[DEC-41]** — see [OQ-83].~~ ⚠ **Amended 2026-08-19 by [DEC-78]** — prices stay quoted and stored ex-VAT **[DEC-26]**, but the amount reserved and later debited is **VAT-inclusive**: `volume × price × (1 + VAT rate)` grossed up at the **[DEC-64]** reference rate. Sized ex-VAT it would under-cover its own debit by the VAT rate, and **[DEC-41]** deliberately has no buffer to absorb that. Still no buffer beyond the VAT gross-up. |
| **Available balance** | Settled balance minus active reservations. The amount a customer can commit. |
| **Correction invoice** | The invoice for the **difference** when metering data changes after a month was finalised **[DEC-99]**. Corrections arrive at any time, months later included **[DEC-98]**, so the monthly run is not a gate that closes: the delta is invoiced whenever it lands. This is the mechanism the annual true-up used to carry, now continuous rather than annual — see [Annual true-up](../40-processes/05-annual-true-up.md). **No materiality threshold**: every difference is handled individually and the €25 default is removed rather than set **[DEC-100]**, which also closes [OQ-76]. Like every invoice it is pushed as a draft and numbered by the **bookkeeping program** **[DEC-88]**. ⚠ Recorded as read: DEC-100's source comment sits on the true-up materiality row but is phrased about deposits and withdrawals, so it may be misplaced in the source; read with **[DEC-99]** it gives one consistent answer — nothing is netted, batched or waived below a threshold. Confirm at the next session. |
| **Invoice number** | The customer-facing identifier of an invoice. ⚠ **Reversed 2026-08-19 by [DEC-88]** — the platform does **not** mint it. It calculates and pushes a **draft**; a human checks it in the **bookkeeping program**; that program assigns the number and issues the document. The platform stores the returned number for display and reconciliation. ⚠ Cost, recorded because **[DEC-45]**'s rationale was exactly this: the customer-facing number now depends on an integration and on a manual check, and a push failure means the customer has no numbered invoice. The **PDF** is generated and emailed by the same program **[DEC-89]**, which also moves document branding out of platform control and closes [OQ-90] — attached or linked is no longer the platform's question. ⚠ How many drafts are pushed per customer per month — one document for the hedge and the day-ahead delivery, or two — is not decided: see [OQ-92]. |
| ~~**Surcharge** / **topup**~~ | ⚠ **Reversed 2026-08-19 by [DEC-73]** — surcharges and topups **leave the platform**. The platform computes and pushes **volume**; the **bookkeeping program** multiplies it by the topup fee. The surcharge tariff table, its resolution order and invoice line 4 are not built, and [OQ-36] (what the surcharge applies to) closes with them. The platform's only margin instrument is the **spread on the price it quotes [DEC-80]**. Original definition, kept readable: *a per-unit adder applied on top of the energy price, configured per customer per period, quoted and stored in €/kWh **[DEC-35]**, applied directly to the kWh volume with no `/1000` divisor, signed so a negative rate is a discount.* The term still appears in the source material as "topup"; this set used **surcharge** in code and UI to avoid collision with *wallet top-up*. ⚠ **The unit trap moves rather than disappears.** With both €/kWh customer rates gone — surcharge here, **feed-in tariff** by **[DEC-87]** — every price the platform holds is €/MWh except the **energiebelasting** bracket rates, which are €/kWh **[DEC-74]**. A €/kWh figure read as €/MWh is still wrong by exactly 1000 while looking plausible. |
| **Wallet top-up** | Adding money to the wallet, by iDEAL **[DEC-58]** or **bank transfer**. ⚠ **Amended 2026-08-19 by [DEC-106]** — bank transfer is a **first-class deposit method**, not an out-of-band manual step: the platform issues a **payment reference** per deposit intent, matches the incoming payment on it, credits the wallet and emails the customer that funds were received. There is **no minimum and no maximum** amount **[DEC-84]**. ~~There is **no route out**: **[DEC-43]** removes the refund payout path, so a surplus balance stays in the wallet.~~ ⚠ **Reversed 2026-08-19 by [DEC-83]** — **withdrawals exist**: the customer raises a request in the portal, PeakPower is notified, an employee pays it out manually by bank transfer to the company bank account on the customer record **[DEC-61]**, and the platform records the request, the approval and the debit. Under four-eyes a withdrawal needs a second admin's approval **[DEC-71]**; a **deposit never does**. **No invoice is raised for a deposit or a withdrawal.** Chargebacks and reversals are the bookkeeping program's **[DEC-85]**. |
| **Payment reference** | The unique code the platform issues for a **bank-transfer deposit [DEC-106]**. The customer quotes it as the payment description; the platform matches the incoming payment on it, credits the wallet and emails the customer. **[DEC-61]**'s IBAN matching is the fallback when the customer omits it. It exists for **wallet deposits only** — invoice payments are matched in the **bookkeeping program** **[DEC-88]**, **[DEC-105]**. ⚠ Which incoming-payment feed the reference is matched against — CAMT.053 import, a PSP webhook, or a SEPA-instant push — is not chosen, and blocks the route: see [OQ-93]. |
| **Four-eyes approval** | ~~The rule that a trade above a value threshold must be **approved by a second account** before PeakPower executes it **[DEC-33]**. ⚠ The **threshold** is reference data with no shipped default — see [OQ-85].~~ ⚠ **Reversed 2026-08-19 by [DEC-71]** — there is **no threshold**, in euros or in megawatts, so the threshold reference table is not built and [OQ-85] closes. Four-eyes is a **per-customer-company mode**: when a company has it **enabled**, a sensitive action taken by one **admin account** of that company must be approved — or declined — by a **different** admin account of the same company. Actions in scope: **add a bank account**, **deactivate a bank account**, **execute a trade**, **add a user**, **withdraw funds**. **Deposits are explicitly out of scope** — a customer can transfer money or use iDEAL on their own, so gating a deposit gates nothing. A bank account **cannot be edited once added**; it can only be deactivated. It is still a condition on identity rather than a permission tree — *the approving account is an active admin account of the owning company and is not the account that acted* — on top of the two-level admin flag **[DEC-71]** amends **[DEC-16]** with. For a trade the gate still sits **after** acceptance, so the reservation is already held; the offer's reaction window is the only clock and both signatures must fall inside it. Refusal is its own terminal state ~~and is open to any active account, including the acceptor, because refusing only ever releases money~~ ⚠ **[DEC-71]** puts the decline in the same hands as the approval — a *different* **admin** account of the same company — so refusal is no longer open to any account. Under **[DEC-111]** the admin who must approve is notified alongside the requester. ⚠ Source tension, recorded rather than resolved: OQ-09's comment omits withdrawal, OQ-85's answer includes it; the union takes withdrawal in and leaves deposits out — confirm at the next session. ⚠ A second tension, recorded rather than resolved: **add a user** is in the action list while **[DEC-16]** — explicitly unchanged on this point — has **PeakPower employees** create customer accounts, so the gate has nothing to bite on until account creation moves to the customer. |
| **Break-glass** | The emergency sign-in path for a small set of **explicitly named employee accounts**, used only when the identity provider is unavailable **[DEC-53]**. It is the one place the platform holds a credential of its own — a password hash, for employees only — and it **amends [DEC-29]** rather than overturning it: no customer account has a platform-held credential of any kind. Accounts are disabled by default, enabled by a second named administrator for a bounded time, second-factored by something that does not depend on the identity provider, alerted and audited on every use, rotated after every use, and **rehearsed on a schedule**. An unrehearsed break-glass path is not a break-glass path. ⚠ The time box and the reachable function set are not yet set — see [OQ-89]. |
| **Non-working day** | A day excluded from the peak calendar. The calendar keeps an exclusion list as reference data per commodity and year **[DEC-14]**, but **[DEC-19]** leaves it **empty** for electricity peak: no day is excluded, public holidays included. The mechanism stays so the answer can change without a release. |

## Integration terms

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **PVNed** | Third-party data provider. Pushes SOAP/XML `TimeSeriesDocument` messages with allocation and imbalance data. ⚠ **Amended 2026-08-19 by [DEC-69]** — PVNed is **a BRP: the first one configured, not the ingestion pipeline itself**. Its webhook, parser and validation path are one adapter behind a port, so a second BRP is additive. It **does** supply reconciliation data after the correction window **[DEC-98]**, sometimes as a manual process. |
| `TimeSeriesDocument` | The PVNed message envelope. See [PVNed integration](../30-integrations/01-pvned-timeseries.md). |
| `Pos` | Position within a period — the 1-based index of a 15-minute interval. `Pos=1` is 00:00–00:15 local time. |
| `BusinessType` / `Direction` / `ProcessType` | PVNed coded fields determining what a timeseries contains. Decoded in the integration spec. |
| **Montel** | Market-data provider supplying price indications and day-ahead prices. **Day-ahead history is available for backfill [DEC-75]**, so positions can be settled retrospectively. ⚠ Integration starts from **an existing PeakPower .NET Montel service** rather than the Montel API directly **[DEC-96]**; its shape and location still have to be read before the estimate is firm. |
| **Ticker** | A Montel instrument identifier for a tradeable product, e.g. Dutch power base month-ahead. ⚠ The symbols for the six products have still not been supplied — [OQ-23] stays open in part, and carries the *bid* vs *ask* wording of the **[DEC-80]** markup with it. |
| **CM.com** | Payment service provider **candidate** for iDEAL deposits. ⚠ **Amended 2026-08-19 by [DEC-86]** — no PSP is chosen, and it is not settled that any PSP is used at all; the provider-agnostic port stands and earns its keep. The **bank-side limit on iDEAL amounts** is the recorded reason bank transfer is a first-class deposit route **[DEC-106]** rather than a fallback. |
| **Bookkeeping program** | The external accounting system — **Odoo, Moneybird or another**, not chosen — that owns what the platform deliberately does not. It **numbers invoices**: the platform pushes a draft, a human checks it, that program assigns the number and issues it **[DEC-88]**. It **generates the invoice PDF and emails it [DEC-89]**. It applies **VAT per ledger account [DEC-76]**, multiplies pushed volume by the **topup fee [DEC-73]**, receives the **energiebelasting** calculation as a ledger entry **[DEC-74]**, and handles **chargebacks and reversals [DEC-85]** and **payment settlement reconciliation [DEC-105]**. It learns about wallet deposits and withdrawals from its **bank feed**, not from the platform **[DEC-109]**; the platform → bookkeeping integration carries **draft invoices and ledger entries only**. Customer records do not exist there yet — the platform creates them, matched on a **stable identifier** carried by both systems, never on name **[DEC-108]**. The **chart of accounts and tax-code mapping do not exist and must be built [DEC-107]**, now with an energiebelasting account and a VAT rate per account, and need a named owner. The financial record of record lives here, not in the platform **[DEC-95]**. See [Odoo accounting integration](../30-integrations/04-odoo-accounting.md); ⚠ [OQ-69] (version and API) gets heavier, not lighter — the platform's invoice cannot be issued without it. |
| **Odoo** | ~~The accounting system that receives finalised invoices.~~ ⚠ **Amended 2026-08-19 by [DEC-88]**, **[DEC-89]** — one **candidate** implementation of the **bookkeeping program**, named in the source alongside Moneybird and "any other program". It receives **draft** invoices and ledger entries rather than finalised ones, and it, not the platform, assigns the invoice number and sends the document. |
| **Entra ID** | Microsoft's identity provider, and **the production identity provider for this platform [DEC-20]**. It owns credentials; the platform never stores a customer password **[DEC-29]**, and the one employee exception is **break-glass [DEC-53]**. Customer MFA is governed by the tenant's policy rather than by the platform **[DEC-51]**; employee MFA is mandatory. ⚠ **Amended 2026-08-19 by [DEC-92]** — customer MFA is **mandatory** too: still enforced by Conditional Access in the tenant **[DEC-66]** rather than implemented in the platform, but no longer optional, and the platform verifies the authentication-method claim on the token rather than trusting the tenant silently. Onboarding friction is accepted. There is **no existing customer-facing identity solution to migrate from [DEC-110]** — greenfield, consistent with **[DEC-56]**. The proof of concept runs with **no authentication at all** — which does not remove the tenancy context pipeline, only the login in front of it. Authentik and Okta were the alternatives considered under [OQ-03]. It runs in PeakPower's **existing corporate Microsoft tenancy [DEC-66]**, which also hosts the Azure subscriptions. ⚠ *Access* to that tenancy is a Phase 0 dependency, not an open question — and **[DEC-67]** puts it on the critical path by running the claim-mapping spike against it. |
| **SendGrid** | The transactional email provider **[DEC-48]**. Carries offer notifications, which are time-critical, ~~and invoices, which **[DEC-47]** puts on the same channel~~ ⚠ **Amended 2026-08-19 by [DEC-89]** — invoices are emailed by the **bookkeeping program**, so **[DEC-47]** still holds (emailed and in the portal) with a different sender. SendGrid narrows to the platform's own notifications: offers, wallet events including the **funds-received** mail on a bank-transfer deposit **[DEC-106]**, and alerts. Offer notifications go to the account that raised the request and, under four-eyes, to both admins **[DEC-111]**, reversing **[DEC-63]** — a 30-minute offer can now die because one person is in a meeting, which is accepted. A dedicated sending domain with SPF, DKIM and DMARC is required and is a lead-time item. |
