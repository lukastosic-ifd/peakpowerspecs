# Assumptions & Decisions

Two registers. **Assumptions** are things this specification takes as true in the absence of a
decision — if an assumption is wrong, the linked work changes. **Decisions** are choices already made
and recorded, with the reasoning kept so they can be revisited honestly.

Open questions live separately in [80-open-questions.md](../80-open-questions.md).

**2026-10-01 — the UI/UX feedback round.** One decision, **[DEC-169]**, taken from sixteen feedback items on the customer portal. It changes where the day-ahead curve comes from (**ENTSO-E**, live and backfilled from 2020, with an optional token), puts a day-ahead card where the Dashboard's hero was, replaces the Prices chart with a grouped **Buy / Sell / EOD** table and a trade-request side panel (**Sell is shown but disabled**), adds two list categories to the trades list, and trims the customer sign-in pages. It **knowingly amends [DEC-81]**: the day-ahead curve becomes exportable and a customer sees one end-of-day price. Every amended statement keeps its text where it stands, marked.

**2026-10-01 — the UI/UX feedback round, second pass.** One decision, **[DEC-170]**, taken from the user's second round of feedback: the sign-in rail is centred, the customer **Trades** page follows the prototype's layout (and the default filter is **All**), the **request-a-trade wizard** is brought to UI/UX practice, and the **back office** takes the same format (auth parity, unspaced EAN, the trade desk, a *Needs your action* section, pills with counts, a dark table head for trade and money tables). It **amends [DEC-169] (8) and (9) and [DEC-167] (18)**; the Trades page matches the prototype's **layout only**. Every amended statement keeps its text where it stands, marked.

**2026-10-02 — the responsive customer portal.** One decision, **[DEC-173]**: the customer portal is responsive (viewport `width=device-width`, a drawer shell below 1024 px, phone / tablet / desktop ranges, one set of layout conventions) and the back office stays a fixed 1280 desktop layout. It amends the scope row "Native mobile apps", [NFR-45], [F03-R25], the desktop persona note and the mockups' excluded breakpoints.

**2026-10-02 — day-ahead prices are ENTSO-E only.** One decision, **[DEC-172]**: the EPEX/DERIVED seed is removed (migration `RemoveSeededDayAheadPrices`), the stored window starts at `DayAheadIngestion:BackfillFrom` (**2026-01-01**) and older rows are pruned by the job, the first tick loads all of 2026, every read stays on the database, and the Dashboard chart is **per quarter-hour** with the 12 cheapest and 12 most expensive quarters marked. It **amends [DEC-169] (1) and (6), [DEC-149] and [DEC-75]**, and **[F08-R15]** and **[F08-R16]** in F08, each marked where it stands.

**2026-10-02 — the onboarding feedback round.** One decision, **[DEC-171]**, taken from ten feedback items on the customer onboarding flow: the wizard's rail shows only the logo, the step counter, the provenance block and the footer hints go, **validate-on-Next** replaces the disabled Next, consent is **accept-by-clicking** (a legal note), and the **bank step can be skipped**. It **amends [DEC-130], [DEC-126] and [F01-R44]**, each marked where it stands.

**2026-09-10 — the multi-business membership round.** One decision, **[DEC-152]**, and it reverses
more standing text than any other row in this register: **nineteen** statements, listed in the row
itself, most amended in place across F01, F13, F12, the domain model, the database design and the
API contracts — three of the nineteen, **[DEC-16]**, **[DEC-71]** and the F12-R39 reversal marker,
are recorded only on the row, not marked in their own file, following the **[DEC-113]** precedent.
The headline is F13 §6's edge case — *"One person works for two customer companies — **Not
supported**"* — which is the feature's entire goal. Taken on the stakeholder's instruction of
2026-09-10, with the cost of its sixth sub-decision put back to them in full and re-confirmed. Every
reversed statement keeps its text where it stands, marked inline or recorded on this row, as the
2026-08-19 round established.

**2026-09-03 — the slice-1 build round.** Twenty-eight decisions, **[DEC-113]** to **[DEC-140]**, taken while
the first slice was being built rather than before it, and recorded here so the register and the build
do not diverge. Three of them reverse standing decisions — **[DEC-113]** takes back [DEC-16] and
[DEC-29], **[DEC-114]** takes back half of [F01-R24], and **[DEC-119]** takes back the identity
provider [DEC-20] and [DEC-03] assumed. **[DEC-119]** is the consequential one: with no access to the
corporate Microsoft tenancy, the platform now owns identity outright, which leaves [DEC-92]'s
mandatory MFA and [DEC-51]'s tenant-enforced policy with nothing enforcing them. Every reversal keeps
the reversed text visible, as the 2026-08-19 round did.

**2026-08-19 — the stakeholder answer round.** Forty-five decisions, **[DEC-68]** to **[DEC-112]**, were
taken in a single pass over the stakeholder answer sheet, and they are the largest correction this
register has taken. **Fourteen earlier decisions are reversed** — [DEC-24], [DEC-30], [DEC-32],
[DEC-33], [DEC-34], [DEC-35], [DEC-39], [DEC-43], [DEC-44] in its second half, [DEC-45], [DEC-46],
[DEC-49], [DEC-57] and [DEC-63] — together with **one assumption**, [AS-12]. Ten further decisions are
amended in place, seven are confirmed or completed, and eight more assumption rows carry a note. None
of it is deleted: every superseded row keeps its text, its number and its reasoning, with the reversal
marked inline, because a register that quietly rewrites itself cannot be audited.

The round's net direction is a boundary being drawn around the platform. **Moved out**: invoicing —
numbering, PDF generation and delivery now belong to the bookkeeping program ([DEC-88], [DEC-89]);
**VAT**, computed there per ledger account rather than by the platform ([DEC-76]); **surcharges and
topups**, of which the platform now pushes only the volume ([DEC-73]); and **payment matching** for
invoices, chargebacks and settlement ([DEC-85], [DEC-105]). **Moved in**: **energiebelasting**, with a
versioned bracket table, per-customer reductions and a ledger push ([DEC-74]), reversing a deferral
that was always going to have to be reversed; and **short selling** ([DEC-72]), which brings market
risk the prepaid wallet does not bound. Two settlement rules changed shape with them: the wallet funds
trading only ([DEC-77]) and metering corrections are invoiced whenever they arrive rather than at an
annual true-up ([DEC-99]).

Each decision below carries its own reasoning. The questions each one closes or opens are marked in
[80-open-questions.md](../80-open-questions.md).

## 2. Decisions

| ID | Decision | Alternatives considered | Rationale |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **DEC-01** | **Modular monolith** for the write-side domain (customers, trades, wallet, invoicing) deployed as two API hosts plus one worker host, rather than fine-grained microservices | Microservice per bounded context | The domain is small, the team is small, and the hardest problems are transactional (wallet + trade state) rather than scale. Aspire makes the multi-host layout cheap to run locally. Microservices can be extracted later along the module seams. |
| **DEC-02** | **Separate APIs for the customer portal and the employee portal**, sharing a domain library | One API with role-based routing | Different auth realms, different exposure (one is public-facing), different rate-limit and hardening profiles. Cheap to do with a shared library; expensive to retrofit. |
| **DEC-03** | **Ingestion is decoupled from processing.** The PVNed webhook persists the raw payload, acknowledges, and enqueues | Parse and process synchronously in the request | PVNed retries on non-2xx. Slow parsing would cause duplicate deliveries. Raw retention also makes replay and dispute resolution possible. |
| **DEC-04** | **Append-only ledger with derived balances**, with a materialised balance row per wallet updated in the same transaction | Balance column only; or event-sourcing the whole domain | Gives an auditable history and O(1) balance reads. Full event sourcing is more machinery than this domain needs. |
| **DEC-05** | **Reservations are ledger-visible but do not change the settled balance.** Each entry records deltas to both settled and reserved amounts, and stores all three resulting balances | Reservations in a side table only | The brief asks for reserved funds to appear as ledger lines with a resulting balance. This satisfies it without double-counting. |
| **DEC-06** | **Trade state changes are events, not column updates.** The current state is a projection of an append-only `trade_event` stream | Status column with an audit trigger | The audit trail is a first-class product requirement ("visible both by customer and employee"), not an afterthought. |
| **DEC-07** | **Interval data is stored per version, with a pointer to the current version per (metering point, delivery date, direction)** | Overwrite on each new document | PVNed sends corrections for up to 10 working days and never revises a document. Keeping versions makes "what did we invoice on" answerable. |
| **DEC-08** | **All timestamps stored as `timestamptz` (UTC); all business days computed in `Europe/Amsterdam`** | Local time storage | The only way DST arithmetic stays sane. Interval position ↔ timestamp conversion is centralised in one calendar service. |
| **DEC-09** | **PostgreSQL only** — no separate time-series database in the first track | TimescaleDB, InfluxDB, ClickHouse | Volume is modest: a 50-EAN customer generates ~3.5 M rows/year. Native declarative partitioning by month plus a BRIN/btree index handles it. Revisit if EAN count reaches four digits. |
| **DEC-10** | **Hangfire with PostgreSQL storage** for scheduled and background work | Azure Functions, Quartz.NET, a hosted queue | Stated preference; keeps the local Aspire story simple; dashboard is useful operationally. |
| **DEC-11** | **Public website is a separate Angular application**, not a route inside the customer portal | Route in the customer portal | Different audience, different caching and SEO needs, and it must stay up when the portal is in maintenance. |
| **DEC-12** | **Money is `numeric(18,6)` in the database, rounded to 2 decimals only at presentation and invoice-line level** | `numeric(18,2)` throughout | Unit prices are €/MWh with sub-cent precision, and volumes are fractional. Rounding early loses money in aggregate. |
| **DEC-13** | **The offer countdown is authoritative on the server.** The client renders a timer; expiry is decided by a server-side job and by a guard on every accept attempt | Client-side expiry | Clock skew and tab-sleep make client timers unsafe for a financially binding window. |
| **DEC-14** | **Peak-hour definition is reference data, not code.** A named calendar per commodity and year, with the working-day rule and the holiday list as data | Hard-coded Mon–Fri 08:00–20:00 | [OQ-02] was unresolved when this was taken, and even now that **[DEC-19]** has settled it the holiday list could change. Making it data means the answer can change without a release. The list is currently **empty** — the mechanism stays, it simply excludes nothing. |
| **DEC-15** | **Gas is modelled as a discriminator from day one** (`commodity` on metering point, product, tariff and price) but no gas logic is implemented. ⚠ **Confirmed 2026-08-19 by [DEC-68]** — gas is out of scope and [DEC-30] is withdrawn, but the discriminator stays for exactly the reason given here | Electricity-only model, migrate later | The column is nearly free now; retrofitting a commodity dimension across interval data, blocks and invoices is not. |
| **DEC-16** | **A customer is a company with one or more accounts, and all accounts of a company have identical privileges.** Accounts are created and deactivated by PeakPower employees only. ⚠ **Amended 2026-08-19 by [DEC-71]** — customer accounts now carry an **admin** flag, so privileges are no longer identical. That flag is the whole of the change: exactly two levels, existing only to make four-eyes expressible. Everything this decision says about *who creates accounts* is unchanged, and the reasoning against maintaining someone else's org chart still holds for anything finer | An intra-company role model (viewer / trader / approver); customer self-service account management | Confirmed by the stakeholder. The customer decides internally who is allowed to do what; encoding their internal governance in the platform would mean maintaining someone else's org chart. The platform's job is to record precisely who acted — which is what makes **[DEC-17]** the necessary counterpart. Closes [OQ-04]. |
| **DEC-17** | **Every customer-initiated action records the acting account**, not merely the company. Trade events, wallet movements and audit records all carry account id, display name and job title | Company-level attribution only | Direct consequence of **[DEC-16]**: if every account can spend the company's money, "the company accepted the offer" is not an adequate record. It also makes the normal split — one person spots the exposure, another approves the spend — legible instead of anonymous. |
| **DEC-18** | **A trade may be started by one account and answered by another.** No requirement that the accepting account is the requesting account | Locking a trade to its originator | The realistic workflow. An energy manager raises the request at 14:25 and the finance director accepts at 14:44; forcing the originator to accept would push customers back to phone calls, which is the behaviour this platform exists to replace. |

### Decisions taken 2026-08-11 — the eleven blocking open questions

These eleven closed the P1 set in [80-open-questions.md](../80-open-questions.md). Several are scoped to a
proof of concept; where that is so, the decision says what the PoC does **and** what must still be built
structurally now so the deferred part drops in later. A PoC decision that quietly hardens into v1 is the
failure mode these notes exist to prevent.
